Curated evidence from graduate work in applied development economics: a Data Envelopment
Analysis (DEA) / Benefit-of-the-Doubt (BoD) master's thesis, a compact DEA-BCC benchmarking
poster, and the thesis defense presentation. All three documents are already finished,
versioned PDFs — this project is documental, not executable; nothing here needs to be run.

## How the two DEA variants relate

Both documents use Data Envelopment Analysis but answer different questions:

- The **poster's DEA-BCC** is a classic output-oriented efficiency benchmark: given fixed
  inputs (GDP, education investment), how much output (enrollment) could a country be
  producing relative to its peers on the frontier?
- The **thesis's DEA/BoD (Benefit-of-the-Doubt)** repurposes the same frontier logic as a
  flexible composite-indicator method: instead of measuring input-output efficiency, it lets
  the data itself choose the most favorable weights for aggregating multiple development
  indicators into a single score, avoiding the arbitrariness of fixed weights.

They are complementary demonstrations of the same underlying technique (linear-programming
frontier methods) applied to two different problems — efficiency measurement and composite
indicator construction.

## Limitations

- Both DEA applications are cross-sectional benchmarks against a specific peer set and
  variable choice; results are relative to that sample, not absolute efficiency claims.
  Frontier position is sensitive to the input/output variables included.
- The poster's 15-country sample and the thesis's own sample were assembled for academic
  purposes at the time of writing and have not been refreshed since.
